MIZAN: Measure-First Adaptive Inference for Energy-Constrained Satellite Payloads

A satellite that runs AI onboard must decide, frame by frame, how much of a small and shifting energy, memory and time budget each inference deserves. MIZAN makes that decision from costs measured on the flight board itself, and keeps correcting them while it runs.

Ahmed Hedhili, Salem Essid, Ahmed Mnasri · Team INTERSTELLAR · SUP'COM, University of Carthage, Tunisia

Paper

The paper

15 pages, IEEE format.

Abstract. We present MIZAN (Arabic for balance), a measure-first adaptive inference system for energy-constrained payloads, and use it to test the three levers such a system has: which model to run, how often to run it, and how much detail to process. MIZAN pairs a calibrated early-exit network at two resolutions with a controller whose safety filter can veto a configuration but never choose one, and which prices energy in closed form from a profiled cost menu, corrected online. Adapting the model saves 52–57 % of per-inference energy on three data-centre accelerators while saturated, and nothing measurable at ten frames per second, because the platform's fixed draw dominates a paced frame. A cost menu profiled on a laptop is 6.5× optimistic on our Raspberry Pi 4B payload. Correcting that menu online cuts mean latency by 31 % and missed deadlines by 49 % on the board. Adaptive inference onboard must measure the platform it runs on, and keep measuring it.

Method

How MIZAN decides

Every frame, the controller reads the satellite's state, proposes the most valuable option it can afford, and lets a safety filter refuse anything that would break a deadline, the battery or the heat limit. What each run really cost is fed back into the menu.

ModelExit depth of a MobileNetV3-Small early-exit network: stop early when confident.
FrequencyRun or skip a frame, guided by its value: clear land beats cloud, ocean or night.
QualityProcess the image at 48 or 64 px.
